## Contribution and evidence

The method controls how repeated rendered descendants of shared source records influence acquisition adaptation. The distinguishing controlled result is containment of collateral alarms on outputs whose candidate pixels did not change. Group weighting, Huber fitting, and median aggregation are established ingredients, not claimed as new general theory.

At the same-panel session-qualified operating point, ordinary robust fitting flags 869/1,792 unchanged observations in altered batches, compared with zero for source balancing. Altered-target detections are 1,790/1,792 and 1,789/1,792 respectively. Both flag 8/800 fresh normal sessions. A source-disjoint pool median is a strong tied alternative and is retained.

The alteration population is reused development evidence, not independent natural defects. Other-panel calibration fails (108/800 normal-session flags). Historical checks give a visible repair tie and a below-tolerance contour correction, not natural-event superiority. The detector scores 128 reference-selected probes, not every image pixel. No intent-label, designer-time, or universal false-alarm guarantee is established.
